# Why IT Security Matters in Financial Compliance

In most industries, IT security is primarily about protecting the company's own systems and data. In financial services, IT security is also a regulatory obligation. Failures in IT security at a financial institution can result in regulatory fines, loss of operating licence, and mandatory reporting to regulators and affected customers.

---

## The regulatory landscape

**GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)** — Applies to any company handling personal data of EU citizens. Financial institutions hold significant volumes of personal data. GDPR requires appropriate technical controls to protect that data, breach notification within 72 hours of discovery, and the right of individuals to have their data deleted. IT teams implement the technical side of GDPR compliance.

**PSD2 (Payment Services Directive 2)** — EU regulation governing payment services. Requires Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) for electronic payments — meaning MFA is a regulatory requirement for customer-facing payments, not just a security best practice. IT teams implement and maintain the MFA infrastructure.

**Central Bank of Ireland requirements** — Financial institutions regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland are subject to IT and operational resilience requirements. This includes maintaining documented IT risk assessments, testing disaster recovery procedures, and demonstrating that IT systems can withstand operational disruptions.

---

## IT controls that directly support financial compliance

**Access management** — Role-based access control, least privilege, regular access reviews. Ensures only authorised people can access regulated data and systems.

**MFA enforcement** — Required by PSD2 for payments and strongly recommended by all financial regulators for internal systems. An MFA deployment that has gaps — accounts without MFA, bypass mechanisms that are not controlled — is a compliance risk.

**Encryption** — Data at rest and in transit must be encrypted. This covers database encryption, laptop disk encryption, and TLS for all web traffic. An unencrypted database containing customer financial data is a breach waiting to happen.

**Audit logging** — Tamper-proof logging of all access to regulated systems. IT teams configure, maintain, and protect these logs. The logs must be retained for the period required by regulation and must be accessible for regulatory inspection.

**Incident response** — Financial regulators require documented incident response plans and mandatory breach notification. IT teams are central to detecting, containing, and documenting security incidents.

**Vulnerability management** — Regular patching, vulnerability scanning, and penetration testing. Unpatched systems in a financial environment are a regulatory finding waiting to happen.

---

## The operational reality

In practice, IT helpdesk teams at financial companies are on the front line of security compliance. Every time you provision a new account, you are making an access control decision. Every time you set up a device, you are determining whether customer data will be encrypted if that device is lost. Every time you complete an offboarding, you are closing an access risk.

Understanding the regulatory context does not change the day-to-day work — you are still resetting passwords and setting up laptops — but it changes how seriously you take the details. In a financial compliance environment, the details matter.
